**TSA is a competitive community of students with an interest in technology. We build, design, and probelem solve. together** You do not need to arrive with any experience, a plan, or even a set interest.

TSA, the **Technology Student Association**, is a national (with some international extensions) nonprofit organization for middle- and high-school students intrested in STEM. It has been serving students through leadership, service, and competition since 1978 (58 years!) JHS TSA is one chapter in that much bigger network. [TSA’s current facts sheet](https://tsaweb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/tsa-facts.pdf?sfvrsn=f9e27923_9) is a nice snapshot of its scale.

## What is an event?

An **event** is a competition with a specific challenge and a published rulebook. Some events ask you to build something. Others ask you to code, make a video, research a problem, design a game, give a presentation, or take a test. You work alone or with teammates as the event allows.

Your work is scored against an objective **rubric**, a checklist of what judges are looking for. The more completely and skillfully you meet that rubric, the higher you place. It is not a popularity contest, and it is not about who has been in TSA longest.

> **A few real examples:** [Artificial Intelligence](./events/Artificial-Intelligence.md) starts with a test on AI concepts, then semifinalists use a GenAI tool to solve an onsite problem. In [Board Game Design](./events/Board-Game-Design.md), a team creates a playable game, including its pieces, instructions, packaging, and a demonstration. [Drone Challenge](./events/Drone-Challenge.md) has teams design, build, document, and fly an open-source drone for the annual challenge.

Browse the [event guide](/) when you are ready to see the actual options. Every event page links to the official rules.

## How competition works

<div class="competition-path" role="list" aria-label="JHS TSA competition path">
  <section class="path-step path-chapter" role="listitem">
    <span class="step-number">01</span>
    <div>
      <h3>Chapter eliminator</h3>
      <p>Some events have a limited number of State spots per chapter. If more JHS members want a spot than we can send, we run an eliminator.</p>
      <p>That might be in the from of a questionaire, a mock run of the event, a test, or a version of the regional qualification. The format depends on the event.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="path-step path-regional" role="listitem">
    <span class="step-number">02</span>
    <div>
      <h3>Regional qualification</h3>
      <p>Your qualifying work is compared with competitors around Washington. This is an intial statewide sweep to see that you are competent enough to compete at a state level.</p>
      <p>For all events, qualification happens online. So you might submit a video showing your <a href="/wa-events/Catapult">catapult launch</a> instead of bringing the catapult to a live competition.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="path-step path-state" role="listitem">
    <span class="step-number">03</span>
    <div>
      <h3>State</h3>
      <p>At State, you do the full event and compete against Washington’s strongest qualifiers. Winning is hard, which is exactly what makes it exciting.</p>
      <p>State is also a conference: meet students from other chapters, attend sessions, and connect with college programs such as the University of Washington Information School and Seattle University.</p>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="path-step path-national" role="listitem">
    <span class="step-number">04</span>
    <div>
      <h3>Nationals</h3>
      <p>Place highly at State and you may represent Washington at Nationals, where chapters from across the country bring their best work. It is a huge competition, college-and-career experience, and a chance to meet an entirely new TSA community.</p>
      <p>In 2027, Nationals is in <strong>Orlando, Florida</strong>, June 23–27. That means you will to Disney World or Universal Studios!</p>

    </div>
  </section>
</div>

<div class="selection-note">
  <h3> We make elimationation solely on merit. Who is most likely to succeed at State?</h3>
  <p>That is the only factor. We do not choose based on how long you have been in TSA, whether you have competed before, who you know, or if you have won in the past. We only care about what you have done this year. A new member can absolutely earn a spot by preparing well and showing the skills the event needs.</p>
</div>

## TSA is not an exclusive commitement

<div class="tsa-fit">
  <div>
    <p class="path-kicker"></p>
    <p>Being in TSA does not mean you have to make TSA your only activity. Many members balance it with band, sports, jobs, other clubs, family, and homework. Pick events and roles that fit your season and your bandwidth.</p>
    <p>You also do not need to attend every meeting. Meetings are there to help you get information, find teammates, and prepare when you are ready. Stay in touch with your team and event deadlines, then take part in the ways that work for you.</p>
